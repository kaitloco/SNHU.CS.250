# SNHU.CS.250
Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC)


Interpreting user needs and implementing them into a program starts with communicating with stakeholders and the Product Owner. Understanding what the customer needs and why they need it helps make sure the development team is working on the right functionality. Reviewing the acceptance criteria, or creating accurate and acceptable acceptance criteria when needed, also helps make sure the requirements are clear and can be properly developed and tested. User stories help by breaking larger requirements into smaller pieces of work and explaining the user's need and the value behind the requested functionality. This gives the Developers and Testers a clearer understanding of what needs to be completed.

Developing programs starts with understanding the Agile and Scrum process and making sure there is clear communication between the Product Owner, Developers, and Testers. Using Scrum and Agile processes, along with SDLC frameworks, provides a structured way to plan, develop, test, and deliver software while still allowing the team to adjust when requirements or priorities change. The SNHU Travel application showed the importance of being able to make those adjustments while continuing development and testing. Processes such as Sprint Planning, Backlog Refinement, Daily Scrums, Sprint Reviews, and Sprint Retrospectives can help keep the team organized and make sure work continues moving forward. Testing throughout development is also important instead of waiting until the end of the project to identify issues.

Being a good team member means collaborating with other team members and being willing to learn from other subject matter experts. It also means having transparent communication and proactively asking clarifying questions when something is unclear or when it could affect the product or software being developed. Different roles bring different knowledge and experience to a project, so being open to feedback and understanding how the different pieces of work connect is important. A good team member should also communicate changes, raise concerns early, and take responsibility for their work. The goal is not only to complete individual tasks but to work together to make sure the product meets the customer's needs.
